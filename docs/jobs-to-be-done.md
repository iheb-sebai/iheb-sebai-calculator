# Jobs to be done

The jobs describe the situation and the outcome people want. They name no app features. Each story in
[user-stories.md](user-stories.md) traces to exactly one job:

| Job | Stories |
|---|---|
| J-1 (Learner): know the daily amount | S-1, S-2, S-5, S-6, S-7, S-8, S-10 |
| J-2 (Learner): know early that I'm behind | S-3, S-11 |
| J-3 (L&D Admin): set a due date people can meet | S-4, S-9 |

## The problem

Companies assign mandatory training with due dates. Learners find out too late that the deadline was not
achievable at the pace they can manage. Admins set due dates without checking that they are achievable. Both
sides are missing the same simple calculation: time left ÷ working days left.

## J-1 (Learner): know the daily amount

> When my company assigns training with a deadline, I want to know how much I must do each working day, so I can
> finish on time without a last-minute rush.

**Done well when:** the learner gets one number they can put in their calendar (minutes per working day). The
number is never rounded down, so following it never leaves them short.

## J-2 (Learner): know early that I'm behind

> When I fall behind, I want to know how late I will be at my real pace, so I can act early (ask for more time or
> time off work to learn).

**Done well when:** the learner sees a plain status, plus a date and a number of days they can quote to their
manager. This includes the learner who has no learning time at all: they need to hear "you won't finish" and
what to ask for. A blank or a technical error does not help them.

## J-3 (L&D Admin): set a due date people can meet

> When I plan a training rollout, I want to set a due date employees can realistically meet, so completion is high
> and I avoid chasing people.

**Done well when:** the admin gets the earliest date that is achievable at the daily time employees really have.
They also get a date with a margin, so one bad day doesn't make the whole group late.
